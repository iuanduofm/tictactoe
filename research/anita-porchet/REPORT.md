# Anita Porchet, Swiss enamellist: a documented history

**Scope:** Biography, training, career, clients, techniques and awards of Anita Porchet (born 1961), as documented in publicly accessible sources up to 26 November 2026.
**Coverage status:** Not verified against the 80% target. No independent second researcher ran a blind search, so no capture-recapture bound was computed. Treat this as a well-sourced single-pass study.

## Summary

Anita Porchet was born on 15 January 1961 in La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland. She learned enamelling from her godfather, a watch engraver, and trained in Lausanne and with the enamellists Elisabeth Juillerat and Suzanne Rohr. She earned her federal engraving and enamelling certificate in 1984. She taught at the School of Applied Arts in La Chaux-de-Fonds in the late 1980s, then went freelance and set up a studio in Lausanne in 1993. Patek Philippe was among the first brands to commission her, and her work has since appeared for Vacheron Constantin, Piaget, Chanel, Hermès and others. She won the 2015 Prix Gaïa in the artisan-creation category and shared the 2017 Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève Special Jury Prize with Suzanne Rohr. She now works from a studio in Corcelles-le-Jorat.

## Disputed points

- **Teaching start.** Watchonista and Wikipedia place her school teaching in 1985–1992; EuropaStar and The Week corroborate about six years. Homo Faber says "she started teaching in 2008". The 2008 date probably refers to later teaching in her own studio, but the Homo Faber page is internally inconsistent ("for the past ten years"), so this stays unresolved.
- **Workshop year.** Robb Report and Wikipedia give 1993 (Lausanne). Homo Faber gives 1994. This report uses 1993.
- **Wikipedia's 1984 date** is used for both the certificate and a Patek Philippe prize. The certificate is confirmed; the prize is single-source.
- **"First woman" to win the Gaïa artisan category.** Quill & Pad implies this from "21 years of (male) winners"; no source states it outright.

## Clients and techniques

Clients named across sources: Patek Philippe, Vacheron Constantin, Piaget, Chanel, Hermès, Chaumet, Audemars Piguet, Jaquet Droz, and Fabergé. The brand primary sites did not yield credits for her (Patek and Piaget search pages carried no mention; Vacheron returned 403), so the client list rests on media sources.

Techniques: cloisonné, champlevé, paillonné (gold leaf specks; she reports stock of early-nineteenth-century paillons), grisaille, miniature painting, plique-à-jour, and Limoges.

Notable works: the Dawn on the Lake pocket watch for Patek Philippe's 175th anniversary; a miniature of the Palais Garnier ceiling for Vacheron Constantin; a 2013 Hermès Mademoiselle Privé Coromandel piece.

## Awards

- Prix Gaïa 2015, artisan-creation category (Watchonista, Quill & Pad).
- GPHG 2017 Special Jury Prize, shared with Suzanne Rohr (official GPHG record).
- Per EuropaStar only: Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie "Hommage au Talent" and a Vaud intangible heritage prize. Not verified elsewhere.
